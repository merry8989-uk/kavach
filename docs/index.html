What Kavach provides

Kavach replaces your dialer, your contacts, your call log and your spam blocker with one fast, good-looking app — then adds something no ordinary dialer has: an AI that can actually pick up and talk for you.

01 — Know who's calling

Never answer blind again

Every incoming call is checked against an on-device database first, then the cloud — so you see a real name, a business, a crowd-sourced spam tag and a risk score before you decide to pick up.

02 — Stay unreachable to spam

Block by pattern, not by accident

Block exact numbers, prefixes, wildcards, regex ranges or whole countries. Silence unknown callers, auto-screen them with AI, or let them ring through — you set the rule, once.

03 — Let AI take the call

Your assistant answers and briefs you

When a call comes in and you're busy, Kavach's AI answers, greets the caller, streams a transcript, works out what they want, texts them back, and sends you a short brief — recording and transcript included.

04 — Keep everything together

Contacts, logs, SMS, in one place

Rich contact profiles with multiple numbers and tags, a unified call log with smart filters, and SMS threads with auto-reply rules — all searchable, all yours, all backed up encrypted.

05 — Made for India

₹ pricing, UPI-style ease, local voices

Indian-English interface, rupee-native formatting, and announcer voices in Indian languages. Built to feel native on the phone you actually use every day.

06 — Private by design

On-device first, consent-gated always

Intelligence runs on your phone wherever possible. Recording and AI-answering are consent-gated, region-aware, and clearly announced to the caller.

Free core, forever. Dialer, contacts, logs, blocking and caller ID cost ₹0. You only pay for the AI assistant and business features.
No account needed to start. Use it as a dialer first. Sign in only when you want encrypted backup, sync or the Pro AI tier.
Everything exportable. Contacts as vCard/CSV, blocklists as rules — your data leaves with you whenever you want.

Questions

Frequently asked

Short answers. For anything else, the Instagram handle at the bottom is the fastest way to reach us.

Is Kavach free?+
Yes. The core — dialer, contacts, call logs, caller ID, blocking, announcer, recording and messaging — is free forever and ad-free. The AI Call Assistant and the business tier are the paid parts (Kavach Pro), priced in rupees and billed through in-app purchase.
Does Kavach reuse Truecaller's data or backend?+
No. Kavach does not reverse-engineer or reuse any competitor's app, database or backend. All intelligence is first-party or on-device, built from our own sources and crowd reports.
How does the AI call assistant work?+
When a call comes in and you've enabled the assistant, Kavach can answer, greet the caller, transcribe the conversation live, classify the intent (delivery, appointment, emergency, sales, spam, personal), follow a dialogue policy, text the caller back if needed, and send you a brief with the summary, transcript, sentiment, risk and the recording.
Is call recording legal?+
Recording and AI-answering are consent-gated and region-aware. Kavach shows a consent banner, announces to the caller when the assistant or recording is active, and gates the feature by region. It's on you to follow the law where you and the other party are located.
Does my data leave my phone?+
Kavach is on-device first. Caller ID and spam scoring try the local database before any cloud lookup, and there's an offline cache. Backups are encrypted. You can export your contacts and blocklists any time.
Which Android versions are supported?+
Kavach is built with Kotlin and Jetpack Compose and targets modern Android, with the default dialer, call-screening and SMS roles. Exact minimum version and per-feature requirements are still being finalised.
How do I request a feature or report a bug?+
Through Instagram at @osintgram_io — it's where feature requests and feedback are discussed. There's a floating button and a footer link on this page too.
Will there be an iOS version?+
Kavach is Android-first, because the default-dialer, call-screening and recording capabilities it depends on are deepest on Android. Not stated for iOS.
